Sometimes Selo cannot tell whether a payment it signed has settled. When that happens it does not guess. It holds the payment as **UNRESOLVED**, stops spending for the job, and leaves the answer to the chain: the operator's `reconcile` command later settles or releases the payment only on proof from the Algorand indexer.

## What an UNRESOLVED payment is

A downstream payment becomes `UNRESOLVED` when a signed payment has left Selo, or may have left it, and Selo has no settlement it can confirm. That happens when:

- your endpoint answered without a settlement transaction id;
- your endpoint claimed a transaction id different from the transfer Selo signed;
- the claimed transaction id is already recorded on another payment;
- your endpoint rejected the signed payment with a `402` (a signed transfer can still be submitted until it expires);
- the request timed out or lost its connection after it left Selo, or a `402` carried an undecodable `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`;
- Selo hit an internal error, or restarted, while the payment was open.

At that moment Selo raises an operator alert, and the job changes in four ways:

| Effect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Budget | The amount moves from `reserved` to `unresolved` and keeps counting against the job budget |
| Spending | The spend guard refuses every further reservation, and `retry_safety` does not replay |
| Verdict | The run records `PAYMENT_UNRESOLVED`, so the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE unless a check already proved a FAIL |
| Report | `money.unresolvedSpendUsdc` shows the amount, and the payment is listed with status `UNRESOLVED` and the `expectedTxId` Selo signed |

See [Payments and spend limits](/concepts/payments#why-ambiguity-stops-spending) for why a timeout never releases capacity.

## Why absence is not proof

An Algorand transaction is valid only up to its **last valid round**. Until the network passes that round, a signed transfer that is not on chain yet can still be submitted and confirmed. Once the network is past it, the transfer can never be confirmed.

So there are exactly two kinds of proof:

- **It settled**: the indexer shows a confirmed transfer that matches the payment.
- **It never will**: the indexer does not have Selo's own signed transfer, and the indexer's current round is past that transfer's last valid round.

Anything else is not proof, and the payment stays held. When Selo signs a downstream payment, it records the id of the transfer it signed and the transfer's last valid round on the payment row, so that both proofs are possible later.

## The reconcile command

Reconciliation is an operator command run against Selo's own database. It never signs, never pays and never changes a verdict.

```bash Terminal
pnpm --filter @selo/api reconcile -- --limit 50
```

| Setting | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `--limit` | How many `UNRESOLVED` payments to examine, oldest first. From 1 to 200; default 50 |
| `DATABASE_URL` | Selo's database |
| `SELO_OPERATOR_MNEMONIC` | Used only to derive Selo's operating address, the expected sender of every downstream transfer. The loaded configuration keeps the address, not the mnemonic |

## How each payment is judged

<Steps>
  <Step title="Wait for the verdict">
    A payment whose job has not reached a verdict is held with `job_active`. Reconciliation runs only after the job has concluded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the network">
    The payment's network must be Algorand Mainnet or Testnet; otherwise it is held with `unsupported_network`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the transaction id">
    Selo looks up the transfer it signed (`expectedTxId`). If no signed id was derived, it falls back to the id your endpoint claimed. With neither, the payment is held with `no_transaction_id` and the indexer is not asked.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask the indexer">
    One `GET <indexer>/v2/transactions?txid=<id>` to `https://mainnet-idx.algonode.cloud` or `https://testnet-idx.algonode.cloud`, with a 10-second timeout, redirects refused and the answer capped at 262,144 bytes. The answer gives the transaction, if known, and the indexer's current round.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide">
    Settle, release or hold, using the table below.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Decision table

| What the indexer shows | Outcome | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A confirmed asset transfer whose asset, receiver, amount and sender (Selo's operating address) all match the payment | **Settle**: `UNRESOLVED` to `SETTLED`, recording the transaction id and the confirmation time | None |
| Selo's signed transfer is absent, and the current round is past the recorded last valid round | **Release**: `UNRESOLVED` to `RELEASED` | None |
| Absent, and the current round is not yet past the last valid round | Hold | `not_found_still_valid` |
| Absent, and no last valid round was recorded, or the id was only claimed by your endpoint and never signed by Selo | Hold | `no_last_valid_round` |
| A transaction that is not an asset transfer, or whose asset, receiver, amount or sender differs | Hold and alert | `conflicting_transaction` |
| A matching transaction without a confirmed round and round time | Hold | `unconfirmed` |
| A confirmed transaction id already recorded on another payment | Hold | `duplicate_tx` |
| A non-`200` status or a failed connection | Hold | `indexer_unavailable` |
| No complete answer within 10 seconds | Hold | `indexer_timeout` |
| An answer that is not JSON, not a transaction search result, too large, or about a different transaction | Hold | `indexer_malformed` |

<Warning>
  A transfer that went to a different receiver, or moved a different asset or amount, never settles the payment. It is held with `conflicting_transaction` and raises an alert for the operator to investigate.
</Warning>

## Each change is one conditional update

A settle or release is a conditional `UPDATE` on the payment row that applies only while its status is still `UNRESOLVED`, plus the matching move of the job's counters, in one transaction:

| Change | `unresolved` | `settled` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Settle | minus amount | plus amount |
| Release | minus amount | unchanged |

The payment keeps the reason it was held: the new resolution reason is appended in front of it, for example `reconciled: confirmed on chain in round <n> as tx <txId>; held because: <original reason>`.

Because each change is conditional, reconciliation is safe to repeat and to run in parallel:

- Two reconcilers on separate connections settle or release a payment exactly once; the other sees it as already resolved.
- A second run after a settlement finds nothing to do and changes nothing.
- A held payment stays exactly as it was across repeated runs.
- A run stopped after reading converges on the next run, and its late write changes nothing.
- If the job's counters cannot move, the payment change rolls back with them.

## Output

```text Output format
reconciling up to <limit> UNRESOLVED payments for <operating address>
<paymentId> job=<jobId> amount=<amount> USDC SETTLED tx=<txId> round=<round>
<paymentId> job=<jobId> amount=<amount> USDC RELEASED absent at round <round>, past last valid round <round>
<paymentId> job=<jobId> amount=<amount> USDC ALREADY_RESOLVED now <status>
<paymentId> job=<jobId> amount=<amount> USDC UNRESOLVED <reason>: <detail>
<paymentId> job=<jobId> amount=<amount> USDC FAILED <detail>
checked=<n> settled=<n> released=<n> already_resolved=<n> held=<n> failed=<n> still_unresolved=<n>
```

The command exits `1` while any payment in the database is still `UNRESOLVED` or any row failed, and `0` once nothing is left to resolve. Every held payment raises a `downstream_still_unresolved` alert with its reason, and every failed row a `downstream_reconcile_failed` alert.

## What you see afterwards

- The private report reads the payment rows each time you open it. A reconciled payment shows its new status, a settled one adds its transaction id to `money.downstreamTxIds`, and `money.unresolvedSpendUsdc` drops.
- The verdict and its reason do not change. A run whose payment was unresolved stays INCONCLUSIVE, because the test itself could not confirm the payment when it ran.

## Payments signed before 26 October 2026

Selo has recorded each signed payment's last valid round since 26 October 2026. Payments signed before then have no last valid round, so reconciliation can settle them on a confirmed matching transfer but can never release them.

## Held inbound payments

Reconciliation covers downstream payments only. When the facilitator does not confirm the outcome of **your** payment to Selo, the job is held INCONCLUSIVE with `FACILITATOR_UNAVAILABLE`, never runs, and answers `502 INBOUND_SETTLEMENT_UNRESOLVED`. Do not pay again for that request. Such a held inbound settlement is still resolved by hand. See [Recover an interrupted run](/guides/resume).
